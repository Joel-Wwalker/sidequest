# Selected exposure forms

`app/components/scene/exposure.ts` names every one of the 506 current substance IDs. An unknown ID throws; there is no default tablet or symbolic-human-use branch. Each entry has a selected form, short explanation and source URL. The registry imports no renderer, timeline or catalog code.

These forms select one scene context. They do not imply that every formulation uses the same route. The 335 catalog entries already marked oral, drink or inhaler retain their established route, with explicit form corrections where the source requires them. The 152 catalog symbolic entries and 19 original entries are individually assigned. No dose, preparation sequence or administration instructions are provided.

The `buccal` rendering family includes oral dissolving forms: its visible label specifies sublingual tablet, gum-adhering tablet, lozenge or orally dissolving tablet. The family name is not displayed as a claim that these all use identical absorption pathways.

Ipamorelin now selects the [investigational IV formulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25331030/) studied after bowel surgery; efficacy was not established. GHRP-2 selects a [human research nasal formulation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4219938/). Neither is assigned an invented subcutaneous route.

Salmeterol, tiotropium and umeclidinium have dry-powder devices; formoterol has a mouthpiece and compressor representing nebulized solution. The other selected respiratory treatments retain metered-dose aerosol devices when supported by their sources.

## Evidence gaps

`research` means the scene examines a closed sample without human administration. It is an explicit selection, never a catch-all for new IDs. A lack of proven benefit is not by itself a reason to choose it: documented nonmedical and investigational routes are shown where supported.

The current source set leaves formulation or route gaps for the following groups. These selections should be replaced when a suitable primary source establishes a form consistent with the authored context; they do not assert that no human use has ever occurred. Documented nonmedical exposure is not assigned a lab-vial scene merely because a substance is unapproved, illicit or risky.

- Peptides: `bpc-157`, `aod-9604`, `ghrp-6`, `kisspeptin-10`, `dihexa`, `emideltide-dsip`, `epitalon`, `ghk-cu`, `cathelicidin-ll-37`, `kpv`, `peg-mgf`, `melanotan-ii`, `mots-c`, `selank`, `semax`, `thymosin-alpha-1`, `thymosin-beta-4-fragment-tb-500`.
- Preclinical or metabolism contexts: `andarine`, `s-23`, `lgd-3303`, `bms-564929`, `pf-06260414`, `tfm-4as-1`, `yk-11`.
- NPS where route uncertainty remains in the reviewed primary material: `o-pce`. It remains a research sample until a source supports a human route and form consistent with the selected context.
- Steroid where human route/form evidence remains insufficient: `mibolerone`. The located route evidence concerns veterinary use, so the registry keeps a research sample rather than implying human use.
- Other selected-context gaps: `european-mistletoe`, `mitragynine`, `ibogaine`.

## Verification scope

The initial regression test reproduced 161 symbolic fallbacks. The registry suite verifies complete catalog coverage, source URLs, selected indication/form pairs, contact sites, immutable specifications and rejection of unknown IDs. Registry checks do not establish visual correctness. Character attachment, assisted delivery, stage wording and full-loop behavior require the independent rendered-scene reviews described in `AGENTS.md`.
